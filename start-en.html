WHY THIS QUESTION MATTERS

Think about it while stopping is still possible.

As AI systems become more capable, autonomous, and embedded in important parts of society and work, decisions and changes may become harder for people to stop or reverse after they are already in motion.

LUMINA-30 is not asking whether society should use AI or reject it. It asks one narrower question: before escalation becomes irreversible, can human refusal, stopping, verification, and redirection still work in practice?

There is a clear time limit for addressing this problem: before AI reaches a point where humans can no longer effectively stop it.

A little more detail

Why now?
As AI enters real-world decisions and automated systems, actions can move faster and through more connected processes than human review. A review that happens after the fact is not an effective “no” if people no longer have the authority or time to stop what is happening.

What should remain possible?
Before an action becomes irreversible, people should still have a real path to refuse, stop, verify, and redirect it. The focus is not formal approval, but whether refusal can still have practical effect.

What is LUMINA-30?
It is an independent public boundary framework for preserving that possibility while progress continues. It is not a certification system, safety guarantee, adopted standard, or substitute for human judgment.
